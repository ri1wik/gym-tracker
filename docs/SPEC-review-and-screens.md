# Review engine, nutrition logic, cardio, library and data model (round 1 synthesis)

Reference material for builders. PLAN.md wins on any conflict; the storage story here (local-first) is superseded by SPEC-cloud.md.

## Feedback engine

- Signal 1, weight trend. Input: one point per week (the chosen-day reading, or the average when the week holds 3 or more readings); slope by Theil-Sen on elapsed days over the last 28 days, as % of body weight per week. Needs 4 readings spanning 21 days; before that the row reads 'Collecting: 2 of 4 weigh-ins. Trend verdict starts at week 4.' Bands for recomp or fat loss: on track minus 0.25 to minus 0.75 (minus 0.25 itself belongs to on track); flat inside plus or minus 0.25, widened to plus or minus 0.4 when weeks hold fewer than 3 readings; too fast beyond minus 1.0 for 2 consecutive weeks; gaining above plus 0.25 for 3 weeks. Lean gain: on track plus 0.1 to plus 0.25 (beginners to 0.5), above 0.5 flags fat gain. Hysteresis: a band changes only when the rate crosses the edge by 0.05 points or sits across it for two weekly runs. GOOD: 'Trend down 0.4% per week, inside the recomposition band. Keep intake where it is.' BAD (too fast): 'Dropping 1.2% per week, faster than muscle can be protected. Add 150 to 250 kcal a day, keep protein at 160 g, keep lifting heavy.' BAD (flat 4 or more weeks, no marker): 'Scale and lifts flat for 4 weeks. Drop 100 to 150 kcal a day or add one 25-minute incline walk, then re-check in 2 weeks.' A flat week on a recomposition goal reads neutral for the first 4 flat weeks, never negative.
- Recomposition marker, a rule not a card: when the trend is flat or slightly up and, in the same 4 weeks, waist is down 0.5 cm or more or at least half the comparable exercises progressed, the weight row becomes a positive: 'Scale flat for 3 weeks, but waist is down 1 cm and 5 of 8 lifts went up. That is recomposition working; change nothing.'
- Signal 2, sessions done. Input: completed strength sessions this week against training days per week from the profile, plus a 4-week rolling value. Speaks from week 1. GOOD at 90% or more: '4 of 4 sessions, third week running.' NEUTRAL at 70 to 89%. BAD under 70%: '2 of 4 sessions. Consistency beats the perfect programme; pick the two days you never miss and protect them.' Extra flag at 7 days with no strength session: 'No lifting for 7 days. Start the shortest template today.'
- Signal 3, working sets per body part. Input: working sets in the rolling week (warm-ups excluded, every working set counts whatever the reps), 1.0 for a primary muscle and 0.5 for a secondary, and a set adds at most 1.0 to a body part (max over its muscles, never a sum); rolled up to the 11 body parts; lower back, front delts, rotator cuff, hip flexors and tibialis earn no credit; band 10 to 20 with an 8-set floor in a deficit; forearms and core are shown but never flagged. Needs 2 logged sessions in the week. GOOD: 'Chest 12, back 14, quads 13, hamstrings 11, shoulders 10, biceps 13, triceps 12: every part in the 10 to 20 band.' BAD (low): 'Hamstrings 4 sets, under the 8-set floor for 2 weeks. Add one RDL or leg curl slot; 3 sets twice a week is enough to start.' BAD (high): 'Chest 26 sets with flat bench numbers. More is not working; drop to 18 and push the reps.'
- Signal 4, protein days hit. Input: days with logged protein at or above 90% of target, out of days logged; needs 4 logged days. GOOD at 70% or more of logged days: 'Protein hit 6 of 7 days, average 158 g against 160 g.' BAD under 50%: 'Protein hit 3 of 7 days, average 118 g against 160 g. Biggest gap is breakfast at 12 g: 250 g Greek yogurt, 4 eggs or 150 g paneer takes it to 35 g.' The engine finds the meal slot with the largest average gap and names a food from the user's own list. COLLECTING: 'Log 2 more days of food this week to unlock the protein signal.'
- Signal 5, cardio minutes. Input: run plus incline plus other minutes this week against the profile target (default 150, band 90 to 300), with running km and vertical metres as detail. Speaks from the second week of use. GOOD: '3 sessions, 130 minutes; incline walking climbed 900 vertical metres against 700 last week.' BAD (low): '40 of 150 minutes. Two 25-minute incline walks after lifting cover the rest.' BAD (high): '320 minutes with 3 hard runs and your lifts did not move. Pull back toward 180 and keep one hard session.' CAUTION (neutral): 'Running km up 30% in one week; hold next week level to protect the knees and the leg sessions.'
- Signal 6, lifts progressed. Input: exercises logged this week that have a previous session; progressed when the best working set carries more weight at the same or more reps, or more reps at the same weight (e1RM by Epley, w x (1 + reps/30), 1 to 10 reps only, reps 1 returns the weight, bodyweight moves use the day's bodyweight plus added load). Needs 4 comparable exercises. GOOD at 50% or more: '6 of 8 exercises beat last session. Bench 60 x 10 to 62.5 x 8.' GOOD (deficit hold): 'Lifts held within 5% for 4 weeks while weight fell 1.5 kg. That is the muscle you kept.' BAD under 25% for 2 consecutive weeks: '1 of 8 exercises progressed, second week running. Use the ready-to-add-weight hint on each card and check the protein row; if protein is fine, take a lighter week.'
- Assembly: each signal returns a status (positive, neutral, negative, collecting), the number, one sentence and one action. The review shows at most 3 positives and 3 negatives in priority order protein, sessions, weight trend, lifts, sets per body part, cardio, then one 'Focus for next week' taken from the top negative's action, or 'Keep doing exactly this' when there is none. Collecting rows sit under 'To unlock' with the exact count still needed, never under negatives, so the week 1 review already speaks through sessions, protein and lifts. The review is a pure function of (records in window, profile, constants); the stored row keeps an inputs hash, seedVersion, weekStartsOn and the user's note, and is recomputed whenever the hash changes, so editing a past set never leaves a stale verdict beside a fresh chart.
- Guardrails in the same module, with tests: calorie floor is the larger of BMR and 1200 kcal (female) or 1500 kcal (male); the deficit never exceeds 25% of TDEE; the rate target never exceeds 1% per week; loss faster than 1.5% per week for 2 weeks always reads 'slow down' whatever the goal; logged intake under the floor on 3 or more days (when calories are logged) triggers a logging check, never praise; banned phrases (burns fat, boosts metabolism, detox, cures, any condition name); no exclamation marks on negatives; every number is named and every message ends in one concrete action; one-line disclaimer on the profile and the review (general fitness information, not medical advice; see a professional if pregnant, under 18, on medication that affects weight, or with a history of disordered eating); a note that the 7700 kcal per kg estimate holds for short windows only. Tone: a deficit week where lifts held is always framed as a win.

## Nutrition logic

- BMR by Mifflin-St Jeor only: men 10 x kg + 6.25 x cm minus 5 x age + 5; women the same minus 161; 'prefer not to say' averages the two. No Katch-McArdle (a bathroom-scale body fat number has a 5-point error and makes the blend worse).
- Activity multiplier means total weekly activity including training (sedentary 1.2, light 1.375, moderate 1.55 'desk job plus 3 to 5 sessions a week', active 1.725); the label says so, because 'outside training' double-counts or under-counts by about 400 kcal.
- TDEE = BMR x multiplier, recomputed weekly from the latest weekly weight. Shown as an estimate with a 10 to 15% typical error; the weight trend is the correction in version 1, adaptive rebasing is later.
- Calorie target by goal: recomp for a beginner, a returning lifter or a higher body-fat start, TDEE minus 15% (band 10 to 20); recomp for a lean or advanced lifter, minus 5 to 10%; lean gain plus 5 to 10%; maintain at TDEE. Rounded to 50 kcal, never below the floor, deficit never above 25%, always a suggestion the user accepts or overrides.
- Protein 2.0 g per kg (adjustable 1.6 to 2.4) on a reference weight capped at the weight that gives BMI 30 for the height, so a heavier friend gets a realistic number. Protein never drops when calories drop; carbs and fat flex and are not shown in version 1.
- Distribution rules the helper applies: 3 to 5 feedings of at least 0.4 g/kg (25 to 40 g); front-load 25 to 30 g at breakfast; one feeding within 2 hours of training; optional 30 to 40 g of slow protein (dairy) before bed; when the gap after 18:00 is over 40 g, suggest two 25 g portions rather than one 50 g.
- Food model: serving-based entries with protein required and calories optional; macros frozen on the meal entry at log time so editing a food never rewrites history; the seed is 40 to 50 staples in the user's own cuisine with per-serving values checked by hand; chips reorder by use.
- 'Protein left today' is the single headline number. The helper is a greedy pick of 2 to 3 foods from the user's list, most used first, that close the gap inside the remaining calories when calories are logged; for example 'gap 48 g: 1 scoop whey plus 200 g Greek yogurt = 45 g, 310 kcal'. Per-25 g reference list by diet pattern: chicken breast 110 g, fish 120 g, 4 eggs or 7 whites, Greek yogurt 250 g, cottage cheese 200 g, paneer 140 g, tofu 250 g, tempeh 130 g, soya chunks 50 g dry, lentils or chickpeas 300 g cooked with a grain, whey 1 scoop, milk 700 ml.
- Calories are a total against target, never scored in version 1 except the under-floor logging check; the weekly budget, calorie adherence, fibre and water come later.
- Worked example pinned as test vectors with exact values: male, 30 years, 178 cm, 80 kg, moderate activity gives BMR 1767.5, TDEE 2740, recomp target 2329 before rounding to 50, protein 160 g, expected loss 0.37 kg per week at the 411 kcal deficit; weekly points 80.0, 79.7, 79.3, 79.0 give a slope of about minus 0.34 kg per week (minus 0.43%), verdict 'on track'; 80 kg x 8 reps gives an e1RM of 101.3 kg. A female and a lean-gain fixture run beside it so every branch is tested, not only the owner's.

## Cardio

- Three forms plus 'other': outdoor run (distance, duration, run type easy, long, tempo or intervals, RPE 1 to 10, optional average HR), treadmill run (speed, incline, duration), incline walk (incline, speed, duration, RPE, optional HR, handrail flag). Everything prefilled from the last entry of that type.
- Derived values: pace in min per km; distance from speed x time on a treadmill; vertical metres = speed km/h x 1000 x hours x incline / 100 (5.5 km/h at 12% for 30 minutes = 330 m, the fixture). No calorie estimate in version 1: the number gets eaten back and vertical metres is the one that keeps rising; when it comes later it uses the walking equation only for 3.0 to 6.0 km/h, the running equation above, hides on handrails, and never feeds the food budget.
- Zone label from RPE: easy (zone 2) 3 to 4, moderate 5 to 7, hard 8 plus. When HR is logged, zone 2 is 60 to 70% of 208 minus 0.7 x age (Tanaka) and the label uses HR. No zone 2 share in version 1.
- Weekly dose: profile target default 150 minutes in a 90 to 300 band, 2 to 4 sessions of 20 to 45 minutes, about 80% easy, at most 1 hard session; incline walking preferred because it interferes least with leg training.
- Incline protocol card: entry 6% at 4.5 km/h for 20 minutes; reference 12% at 4.8 km/h for 30 minutes, hands off the rails, short sentences possible; scale-up one lever per week in this order: incline by 2% per week to 12, then duration to 30 and 45, then incline to 15, then speed 4.8 to 5.3 to 5.6 km/h, then a weighted vest rather than more speed; scale-down on tired legs 8% at 4.8 km/h for 20 minutes; if the rails are needed, drop the incline.
- Running rules card: weekly km rises at most 10%, every 4th week drops 30%, the long run is at most 30% of weekly km, one hard run a week, easy means full sentences at RPE 3 to 4; beginners start walk-run (1 minute run, 2 minutes walk, 20 to 30 minutes, three times a week) and move on once 20 minutes nonstop is comfortable. The 12-week schedule stays a reference card in version 1.
- Placement rules baked into the templates: lift first on a shared day or keep 6 hours between; no hard run in the 24 hours before a heavy leg day; incline walks after an upper day or on a rest day, and if they must share a leg day, after the lifting and capped at 20 minutes at 8%; the long run before a rest or upper day; at least one full rest day; in a deficit no more than three hard sessions a week counting heavy lower days.
- Progress measures: weekly minutes split run and incline, easy-run pace at the same RPE over 6 weeks, weekly running km, weekly vertical metres; the review's cardio signal reads minutes against target and flags a running km jump above 15% in one week.

## Library

- One seed file, src/data/seed/exercises.json: the 85 exercises already written in the library pass across the 11 body parts, each with id (stable slug), name, aliases, bodyPart, primaryMuscles, secondaryMuscles (capped at two), equipment, movementPattern, isCompound, isUnilateral, loadType (weight, bodyweight, assisted, time), repMin, repMax, incrementG, restSec, one original cue, isRecompPriority, demoImage null. Nothing scraped; names are generic movement names; no images in version 1.
- Muscle contract written before M1: 22 mapping muscles in the seed, a fixed table from muscle to the 11 browse body parts (the 14-group scoring level stays in the table for later), and the roll-up rule 'a set adds at most 1.0 to a body part, max over its muscles'. A Vitest fixture runs both templates through the volume function and pins the recomputed totals (for example 4-day: chest 10, back 14, biceps 13), and asserts every flagged part of the default template sits at 8 or above, so the gauge can never flag the app's own default.
- Secondary audit before the seed ships: Back Squat loses hamstrings, Trap Bar Deadlift keeps at most two secondaries, Overhead Press loses upper_chest; aliases cover RDL, OHP, pulldown and the like; the two Dips and the two RDLs keep distinct ids.
- Browse: 11 body parts, priority exercises first, search by name and alias, equipment filter that hides what the gym does not have, and a 'Start a session with these' button that seeds a session from that part's priority exercises. Custom exercises require at least one primary muscle.
- Two programmes in src/data/seed/templates.json referencing exercise ids: 3-day full body (A, B, C) and 4-day upper/lower (Lower A quad focus, Upper A horizontal, Lower B hinge, Upper B vertical) with sets, rep ranges, rest and a cardio placement note per day; the 4-day is the default. Push/pull/legs, dumbbell-only and bands-only come later.
- Double progression everywhere: rep ranges 5 to 8 on the heaviest compound, 8 to 12 on the second, 10 to 15 on isolation; the logger prefills the last load and reps and raises the 'ready to add weight' flag (increment prefilled next time) when every working set reaches the top of the range. Increments are editable per exercise because machine stacks and dumbbell steps vary by gym.
- Foods seed, src/data/seed/foods.json: 40 to 50 staples in the cuisine the user confirms, per serving (serving label, protein, kcal, optional carbs and fat), checked by hand against packaging or a standard table; everything editable; the first week of logging reorders the chips.
- Seed versioning: seedVersion in Meta; on first launch import with isCustom false; on an app update with a higher seedVersion, upsert by slug, skip rows where userModified is true, never delete custom rows. A ten-line Vitest checks unique ids and known muscle and equipment enums.
- Scheduling guidance ships as text inside the templates and the two cardio cards, not as a calendar: Home lists templates by least recently done and the user taps the one they are doing.

## Data model (local, superseded by SPEC-cloud.md for server columns)

- Common: id (crypto.randomUUID, or a stable slug for seeded rows), createdAt, updatedAt (ISO); day-keyed rows carry date as a local YYYY-MM-DD key; integers in canonical units (grams, millimetres, metres, seconds, kcal); no soft deletes in version 1
- Profile (single row 'me'): displayName, sex, birthDate, heightMm, activityLevel, goal, trainingAge, trainingDaysPerWeek, cardioTargetMin, proteinGPerKg, calorieTargetKcal (computed) and calorieOverrideKcal, dietPattern, weighInWeekday, weekStartsOn, onboardingDone
- Exercise: id, name, aliases[], bodyPart, primaryMuscles[], secondaryMuscles[] (max 2), equipment, movementPattern, isCompound, isUnilateral, loadType, repMin, repMax, incrementG, restSec, cue, isRecompPriority, isCustom, userModified
- WorkoutTemplate: id, name, daysPerWeek, days[] of { name, cardioNote, items[] of { exerciseId, sets, repMin, repMax, restSec } }
- WorkoutSession: id, templateId?, dayName, startedAt, endedAt?, status (in-progress, completed), bodyweightG snapshot, notes
- SetEntry: id, sessionId, exerciseId, setIndex, setType (warmup, working), weightG, reps (seconds for timed moves), completedAt; e1RM, volume and progressed flags are derived, never stored
- CardioSession: id, date, type (run-outdoor, run-treadmill, incline-walk, other), durationSec, distanceM?, speedMPerH?, inclineTenthsPct?, rpe, avgHr?, runType?, handrail, notes; pace and vertical metres derived
- WeighIn: id, date, weightG, waistMm?, note; one per day, latest wins
- FoodItem: id, name, servingLabel, proteinDg (tenths of a gram), kcal?, carbsDg?, fatDg?, useCount, lastUsedAt, isCustom, source (seed, user)
- MealEntry: id, date, slot (breakfast, lunch, dinner, snack), foodItemId?, name, servings, proteinDg, kcal?, loggedAt; macros frozen at log time
- WeeklyReview: id, weekStart, weekStartsOn, inputsHash, seedVersion, signals[] of { code, status, number, headline, action }, focus, userNote, generatedAt
- Meta: schemaVersion, seedVersion, lastExportAt, installedSeenAt
- Dexie indexes: sessions by startedAt and status; sets by sessionId and by [exerciseId+completedAt]; weighIns, meals and cardio by date; reviews by weekStart

## Screens

- First run: In-app browser gate, then one welcome line ('your data stays on this device'), the iOS 'Share then Add to Home Screen' banner or the Android install button, an 'Import a backup' link, a one-screen profile, pick a template, and the line 'weight verdicts start at week 4'.
- Home: Templates sorted by least recently done with a Start button, protein left today, the weigh-in banner on the chosen day, the weekly review card when it is ready. No calendar, no scheduled 'today's workout'.
- Train: Three segments: Program (the two templates), Library (11 body parts with the priority exercises first, alias search, equipment filter, 'Start a session with these'), History.
- Active Session: Full-screen takeover with the tab bar hidden: elapsed timer, one expanded exercise card showing 'Last time' and 'Best', set rows prefilled with weight, reps and a large check, plus and minus steppers, rest timer bar with the end time, add-exercise sheet, Finish.
- Session Summary: Duration, sets, volume, body parts worked, progressed / same / lower per exercise, and the 'ready to add weight' hints for next time.
- Exercise Detail: The cue, last three sessions, best set, top-set and e1RM chart, editable increment and rest time.
- Cardio Log: Outdoor run, treadmill run, incline walk and 'other' forms prefilled from the last entry, with the incline protocol card and the running rules card beside them.
- Food: Protein left today as the headline, calories second, meals by slot, frequent-food chips, the Add Food sheet (chips, search, create, quick macros), copy yesterday, and the 'Hit your protein' helper.
- Weekly Check-in: Step 1: weight prefilled from last week with the same-conditions text and an optional waist field. Step 2: the review with positives, negatives, focus for next week, 'to unlock' rows and a note field.
- Progress: Weight chart with weekly points, trend line and band; sets per body part against the 10 to 20 band; weekly cardio minutes split run and incline; per-exercise lift chart; the check-in list with deltas.
- You: Profile and computed targets, template choice, weigh-in weekday, installed-or-not badge, share-sheet export with the last export date, replace-only import, the disclaimer.

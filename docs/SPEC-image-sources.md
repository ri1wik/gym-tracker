# Image sources and licences

Reference material for builders. PLAN.md wins on any conflict.

## Recommendation

Do not ship the free-exercise-db photos. Its LICENSE.md is the genuine Unlicense text, but that licence only covers what the author owned: the upstream repo (wrkout/exercises.json, CONTRIBUTING.md) states the 1,746 images were scraped off the internet and the author does not own the copyright; the model in Leverage_Incline_Chest_Press/0.jpg wears a Bodybuilding.com shirt; Bodybuilding.com's terms forbid reuse; and the maintainer (issues #2 and #13, latest reply 2 Aug 2026) says he has no idea where the images came from and is discussing swapping them for placeholders. For v1 use two layers. Layer 1, exercise demo images: the RepDB free tier (github.com/sergei-argutin/exercise-dataset): 609 exercises, 1,082 original 512x512 flat WebP illustrations (start and peak pose), 92 machine or cable exercises with specific machine taxonomy, free for commercial in-app use with one visible credit line, bundled in the repo at two sizes (about 4.8 MB for 85 exercises). Layer 2, machine identification: a one-hour visit to the user's Anytime Fitness with a phone, two angles per machine, which is the only legal way to show the exact Hammer Strength and selectorised units he is confused by (manufacturer product shots are copyrighted and their terms forbid reuse; photos you take of a machine are yours). Path to higher quality later: RepDB Standard ($499 one-time: looping animations, transparent backgrounds, one consistent character, no attribution) or Gym visual per-asset licences (under $0.75 per illustration, $0.90 per GIF, app use allowed), with AI-generated or Blender renders only if a unique style matters more than the time.

## yuhonas/free-exercise-db: what it actually is

- Counted on disk: 876 exercises (README says 800+), 873 with exactly two images (0.jpg = start, 1.jpg = end position), 3 with none; 1,746 JPEGs, 98.7 MB, median 53 KB; 80% are 850x567 progressive JPEG, the rest 850x569, 500x750, 750x500, 850x1275. Usable at phone size, but busy gym backgrounds and other people in frame.
- Equipment enum: barbell 170, dumbbell 123, other 122, body only 111, cable 81, null 77, machine 67 (58 in strength), kettlebells 56, bands 20, medicine ball 17, exercise ball 12, foam roll 11, e-z curl bar 9. The 67 machine entries include leg press, hack squat, leg extension, lying/seated/standing leg curl, Leverage (Hammer Strength) chest/incline/decline press, iso row, high row, shoulder press, shrug, deadlift, butterfly, machine bench press, preacher curl, triceps extension, dip machine, ab crunch machine, calf machines, thigh abductor/adductor, 18 Smith machine variants, 9 cardio machines.
- JSON fields (schema.json): id (slug), name, force (push/pull/static/null), level, mechanic (compound/isolation/null), equipment, primaryMuscles[] and secondaryMuscles[] from a 17-value enum (abdominals, abductors, adductors, biceps, calves, chest, forearms, glutes, hamstrings, lats, lower back, middle back, neck, quadriceps, shoulders, traps, triceps), instructions[] (numbered steps), category, images[] (paths relative to exercises/). dist/exercises.json is the combined array; images can be hot-linked from raw.githubusercontent.com.
- Provenance: fork of wrkout/exercises.json (Ollie Jennings, 2020, Unlicense). That repo's CONTRIBUTING.md says the images were scraped off the internet, the author does not own the copyright and advises against commercial use; reverse image search (wrkout issue #305) points at Bodybuilding.com, whose terms grant no right to reproduce or distribute content; one commenter also identifies ExRx.net photos. The Bodybuilding.com shirt in the Hammer Strength incline press image settles it visually.
- Maintainer's own position: issue #2 (June 2023) "I actually have no idea where the images are from or if they are royalty free so usage would be at your own risk"; issue #13 is still open with June to August 2026 calls to remove the images and a maintainer reply about replacing them with placeholders. Sharing the app with friends on a public GitHub Pages URL is public distribution, so the images are out.
- The instruction text also carries Bodybuilding.com house phrasing ("This will be your starting position", "Repeat the movement for the prescribed amount of repetitions"), so treat the prose as doubtful too. The structured fields (names, equipment, muscle tags, force, mechanic) are facts, not expression, and are safe to use as a cross-check when tagging the 85 exercises.

## wger and Everkinetic (CC BY-SA): what share-alike means here

- 277 English-named exercises have at least one image. wger's equipment taxonomy has no machine category (only Cable machine, 32 exercises); machine exercises exist with the equipment field blank (Leg Press, Leg Extension, Leg Curls sitting/laying/standing, Shoulder Press on Machine, Butterfly, Chest Press, Machine chest fly, Hammerstrength Decline Chest Press, Leg Press on Hackenschmidt Machine). Against the 85-slug library only 53 map cleanly and 23 have no match (notably lat pulldown variants, cardio machines, hack squat, dip machine, cable crunch, elliptical, rower, jump rope). Quality is uneven: user selfies and clipart side by side.
- Everkinetic: the site is offline; the drawings survive on Wikimedia Commons (search "everkinetic" in the File namespace returns about 1,089 hits: PNG originals at 221x275 px, SVG re-traces, and a few GIFs), each tagged CC BY-SA 3.0, Artist Everkinetic, credit http://everkinetic.com/. Two-frame line drawings. Machine coverage is real but thin: Smith machine family (squat, bench, incline, shrug, upright row, calf raise, deadlift, good morning, hack squat), butterfly machine, machine bench press, shoulder press machine, hack squat, lying leg curl machine, bicep curl machine, preacher curl machine, triceps extension machine, calves press on leg machine. The SVGs fix the low resolution.
- Share-alike consequences for this app: the app code and database stay under whatever licence you choose (an image inside a collection is not a derivative of the app). Any modified image (crop, recolour, composite, added muscle overlay) must be released under the same CC BY-SA licence and marked as modified. Every image needs a visible credit with author, licence name and link, and source link, which means per-image licence metadata in the exercise JSON and an Image Credits screen. Thumbnails and resizing are fine. Workable for a friends app, but the bookkeeping plus the mixed quality is why these are the fallback, not the primary.
- wger's own exercises data is also CC BY-SA 3.0 (its README), so copying wger instruction text would pull the same share-alike and attribution duties onto the text. The exercemus/exercises repo is a convenience layer over the same wger data with per-exercise licence fields; same terms apply.

## API and paid sources: ExerciseDB, MuscleWiki, Gym visual

- ExerciseDB (now AscendAPI, exercisedb.dev): V1 is free with 1,500 exercises and GIFs and needs no key; V2 is a RapidAPI subscription (11,000+ exercises, 5,000+ GIFs). The ExerciseDB API Terms of Use (Notion) state that all content including GIFs is the exclusive property of AscendAPI, the licence is revocable and limited to the subscription period, and a Strict Storage Prohibition clause requires every render to fetch fresh from the API; scraping and bulk collection are forbidden. A one-time Gumroad dataset (about $300, tiers 180p to 1080p) advertises commercial rights but publishes no caching clause, and a May 2026 developer thread could not confirm where the GIFs originate. Verdict: cannot be bundled in a repo or precached by a service worker, so it breaks offline use; not for v1.
- MuscleWiki API (api.musclewiki.com/api-terms, dated 28 Jan 2026, supersedes the 14 Nov 2025 RapidAPI version): no downloading, exporting or storing of videos, thumbnails or bodymap images (section 2.1); no re-hosting on your servers or CDN (2.2); thumbnails cacheable 24 hours, metadata 30 days, videos transient only, permanent storage prohibited (3); legal disclosure required ("Exercise data and videos provided by MuscleWiki.com") and embedded MuscleWiki branding must not be removed (8); plans and quotas via their dashboard (about 500 calls per month free per third-party listings). Streaming only; useful later as an optional "watch video" link, never as the bundled image.
- Gym visual: the hasaneyldrm/exercises-dataset repo (1,324 exercises, MIT data) redistributes Gym visual thumbnails and GIFs with permission, capped at 180x180 and requiring the notice "© Gym visual" with the link https://gymvisual.com/ on every use; cloning grants no further licence. Gym visual's own terms allow purchased media in iOS/Android apps and websites, perpetual, one-person licence, no redistribution, no attribution clause; price under $0.75 per illustration after 10 in cart, $0.90 per animated GIF, $6 per video. 85 GIFs is roughly $77; start-and-end illustration pairs for 85 exercises roughly $130.
- WorkoutLabs, Muscle & Motion and other illustration libraries require a written commercial licence for apps; not free, not evaluated further.

## RepDB free tier: the v1 primary source (verified on disk)

- Style: clean flat vector-look illustration of one consistent male figure on a pale background, machine geometry drawn plausibly (verified on pec-deck-peak.webp, a selectorised pec deck with weight stack). Readable at 256 px on a phone. Equipment icons at 128 px are good as list icons, too small for a hero image.
- Fields per exercise: id (kebab slug), name_en/de/es, description_*, category, force_type, mechanic, difficulty, equipment (specific slugs such as leg_press, hack_squat, pec_deck, lat_pulldown_machine, shoulder_press_machine, plate_loaded_lateral_raise_machine, hip_thrust_machine, smith_machine, cable, treadmill, rower), body_part, primary_muscles and secondary_muscles (anatomical names such as rectus_abdominis, latissimus_dorsi), goals, tags, is_unilateral, is_bodyweight, instructions_* (steps), tips_*, MET values. 92 machine or cable exercises with images, including 19 Smith machine variants and 6 leg press variants.
- LICENSE-DATA.md v1.0 (read in full): 1) free for personal and commercial use inside applications; 2) attribution required, a visible link "Exercise data by RepDB (repdb.co)" in the about/credits screen, README or footer; 3) no republishing, reselling or repackaging as a dataset or API; 4) resize, crop and recolour allowed for in-app use, upscaled or background-removed derivatives fall under rule 3; 5) no use of the images as input or reference for generative AI; 6) premium-samples/ folder is evaluation only, never ship it; 7) no warranty, not medical advice. Origin statement: all images are original works generated with AI tooling commissioned by RepDB, not derived from any third-party set. Licensor is a named individual in Berlin with a contact address, which is the clean provenance chain the other free sets lack.
- Paid tiers (repdb.co/pricing, one-time, VAT included): Starter $299 (still images, 1024 px classic style), Standard $499 (looping animations for all 609, transparent backgrounds, highlighted muscles, heatmaps, JSON, no attribution required), Female add-on +$99, Custom from $999 (white-label, bespoke style, extra exercises). This is the natural upgrade when the app wants animated demos.

## Machine photos: what is legal

- Photos you take of a machine are your own copyright. A gym machine is a useful article, not a protected artwork, and photographing it infringes nothing. A Hammer Strength, Life Fitness, Technogym, Precor or Cybex logo visible in your photo is incidental and descriptive; the trademark risk only arises if the app uses those marks as its own branding or implies endorsement. Do not put a manufacturer or Anytime Fitness logo in the app icon, name or marketing; a logo on the machine in a demo photo is fine. Optionally blur logos for a cleaner look.
- Manufacturer product images are out. Life Fitness Terms of Use section 10: site content including images and logos is the exclusive property of Life Fitness and its content providers, copying is permitted only for personal non-commercial use with notices intact, any other use is expressly prohibited and no trademark licence is implied. Technogym, Precor and Cybex carry equivalent clauses. Scraping catalogue shots, press kits or dealer sites is infringement regardless of the app being free.
- Anytime Fitness has no published chain-wide photography rule; clubs are franchised and the NZ Membership Guidelines 2024 say policies vary and to ask the club owner. Gym contracts generally treat filming other members as a breach. Practical rule: ask the front desk or owner once, shoot during a quiet hour (24-hour access helps), keep every other member out of frame, never near changing rooms. No model release is needed when nobody is in the frame; if the user is in his own photos none is needed either; a friend demonstrating needs a simple consent, written if the app ever goes beyond friends.
- One-hour shoot plan: a checklist of the roughly 35 machines the library uses, two frames each (full machine from the approach side, then the seat, pad and handle setup with the pin visible), landscape 4:3, same phone height, flash off. Name files by machine slug the same evening. Add a free-text setup note per machine (seat height, pin, handle choice) because that is what the user is actually confused about when he stands in front of it.
- Store machines as their own entity, not as an exercise attribute: machines.json with slug, plain descriptive name ("plate-loaded iso-lateral row", no brand needed), photos[], setup notes, and exercises reference machineSlug. That is what makes "which machine do I use for this" a one-tap answer and lets friends at a different gym swap in their own photos later.

## Generating original images instead

- 3D renders: Blender with a free rigged character and modelled machines gives the most consistent set and clean transparent backgrounds, but modelling 35 machines is days of work; only worth it if the app's look becomes a product. RepDB Standard delivers that consistency for $499 and zero modelling.
- Own photos with the user as model: cheapest high-fidelity option for machines specifically; pair each machine photo with the RepDB illustration for the movement so the app shows the real machine and a clean demo side by side.

## Mapping work for the 85 slugs

- Result: RepDB matched 79 of 85 directly; 4 clear misses that need a proxy or an own photo: the Hammer Strength plate-loaded incline press and iso-row, machine high row, sled push (RepDB has sled row only); 2 to confirm by hand: glute-ham raise (RepDB has a glute_ham_developer equipment entry) and farmer's walk. free-exercise-db text matched 80 of 85 (useful only for cross-checking tags). wger matched 53 of 85 and is the weakest fit.
- Store the mapping explicitly: each exercise record carries media: { source: "repdb", id: "<repdb id>", start: "img/ex/<slug>-start", peak: "img/ex/<slug>-peak" } and machineSlug. Keep a media-manifest.json listing every shipped file with source, licence, author and URL so a second source (own photos, a later CC BY-SA drawing) can be mixed without losing track of attribution duties.
- Build step: a Node or Python script that reads the mapping, copies the matched RepDB files, and runs cwebp to produce two sizes (256 px at q80, 512 px at q82) into public/img/ex/. Measured on six machine images: 256 px averages 7.2 KB, 512 px averages 21 KB. Use <img srcset> with 256w and 512w plus loading="lazy"; the service worker precaches the 256 set and fetches 512 on demand.

## Storage layout and size estimates

- Bundled in the repo under public/img/: 85 exercises x 2 poses at two sizes = 170 x (7.2 + 21) KB, about 4.8 MB; the 35 or so equipment icons at 128 px add about 0.25 MB. Well inside GitHub Pages limits (1 GB repo soft limit, 100 MB per file) and small enough to precache for offline use. Illustrations belong in the repo because they version together with the exercise JSON and need no backend.
- User's own machine photos: shoot at full resolution, keep the originals off the repo, ship an 800 px WebP (about 60 KB) and a 1600 px WebP (about 180 KB) per frame; 35 machines x 2 frames is roughly 17 MB, still fine in the repo if the set is fixed, but put them in the backend bucket (Supabase Storage 1 GB free, Cloudflare R2 10 GB free, Firebase Storage 5 GB free) as soon as friends can add photos of their own gyms, because per-user uploads cannot live in a public repo and the bucket gives signed URLs and per-user privacy.
- Never hot-link raw.githubusercontent.com or wger.de for production images: provenance aside, both are outside your cache control and the free-exercise-db images may be deleted (the maintainer is discussing placeholders).

## Attribution text to ship

- Required for v1 (RepDB free tier), visible in the About/credits screen and in the README: Exercise data by RepDB (repdb.co), linked to https://repdb.co. HTML form from ATTRIBUTION.md: Exercise data by <a href="https://repdb.co">RepDB (repdb.co)</a>. Resizing to 256 and 512 px is allowed; do not ship anything from premium-samples/.
- If any wger or Everkinetic image is ever used: a per-image line such as "Image: Everkinetic, CC BY-SA 3.0, via Wikimedia Commons" with links to the file page and to creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/3.0/, plus "modified" when cropped or recoloured, and the modified file released under the same licence. If any wger community photo is used, author from license_author and the licence from the license field (1 = CC BY-SA 3.0, 2 = CC BY-SA 4.0); skip images whose author field is blank.
- If Gym visual thumbnails from the hasaneyldrm set are ever used: the notice "© Gym visual" with the link https://gymvisual.com/ on every image, at 180x180 only. If MuscleWiki streaming is added later: "Exercise data and videos provided by MuscleWiki.com" in the Terms or Privacy page.
- Own photos: no attribution needed; add a one-line statement in the About screen that machine photos were taken by the author at his own gym and that manufacturer names are used descriptively.

## Must have

- Replace every free-exercise-db image before anything is pushed to the public GitHub Pages URL; the images are scraped Bodybuilding.com and ExRx photos and the Unlicense does not cover them.
- Adopt the RepDB free tier as the v1 exercise image source: clone github.com/sergei-argutin/exercise-dataset, map the library to RepDB ids, ship start and peak frames at 256 and 512 px WebP under public/img/ex/.
- Ship the RepDB credit line "Exercise data by RepDB (repdb.co)" linked to https://repdb.co in the About/credits screen and the README.
- Model machines as a separate entity (machines.json with slug, plain name, photos, setup notes) and give every machine-based exercise a machineSlug; the machine photo and the exercise illustration are two different answers to two different questions.
- Photograph the user's own Anytime Fitness machines (two frames each, quiet hour, nobody else in frame, owner asked once) for the Hammer Strength and selectorised units that no illustration set identifies; never download manufacturer product shots.
- Keep a media-manifest.json with source, licence, author and URL for every shipped image so mixed sources stay attributable.
- Delete the premium-samples/ folder from the copied RepDB assets; it is evaluation-only under licence term 6.

## Later

- Upgrade to RepDB Standard ($499 one-time) when animated demos are wanted: looping animations, transparent backgrounds, one consistent character, no attribution, same ids so the mapping carries over.
- Alternative paid path for a handful of exercises: Gym visual per-asset licences (under $0.75 per illustration, $0.90 per GIF), app use allowed, no attribution.
- Optional streaming video layer via the MuscleWiki API (no storage, 24-hour thumbnail cache, disclosure line in the Terms page) as a "watch" button, never as the bundled image.
- Fill the 4 to 6 RepDB gaps (plate-loaded incline press, iso-row, machine high row, sled push, verify glute-ham raise and farmer's walk) with own photos or AI line drawings generated from own photos, not from RepDB images.
- Move machine photos to the backend bucket (Supabase Storage, R2 or Firebase) once friends can upload photos of their own gyms; keep illustrations in the repo.
- If a unique visual identity becomes a goal, build a Blender or LoRA-based set from the user's own photos; budget days, not hours.

## Risks

- free-exercise-db images are infringing (upstream author's own statement, Bodybuilding.com shirt in the photos, maintainer discussing removal); shipping them on a public URL is distribution even for a friends-only app, and the hot-link source may vanish.
- free-exercise-db instruction text shows Bodybuilding.com house phrasing; copying it carries the same risk as the images, so use RepDB's instructions or write your own.
- RepDB free tier is a custom licence, not CC: in-app use only, no redistributing the set or an API built on it, no use as AI reference material, attribution must stay visible; forking the app repo with the images inside is fine, publishing the images as a dataset is not.
- CC BY-SA images (wger, Everkinetic) pull share-alike and per-image attribution duties onto any modified image; 99 wger images have no recorded author and cannot be attributed correctly.
- ExerciseDB and MuscleWiki terms forbid storing media, so neither works offline or in a static bundle, and ExerciseDB's GIF provenance is unconfirmed.
- Manufacturer product images (Hammer Strength, Life Fitness, Technogym, Precor, Cybex) are copyrighted and their terms forbid reuse; only photos the user takes himself are safe, and manufacturer or Anytime Fitness logos must stay out of the app's own branding.
- Gym photography rules are set per Anytime Fitness club; photographing other members breaches most membership agreements, so shoot empty machines only.
- Pure AI-generated images have uncertain copyright protection; no third-party claim risk if prompts avoid brands and living artists, but the app cannot stop others copying them.
- The 85-slug mapping was built on a stand-in list because the project folder holds no exercise library yet; the real list may shift the miss count by a few.

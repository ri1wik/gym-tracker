# Retention: version 1 priority list

Pass 1 has the right spine (a 20-second logging loop, a Sunday review built from the user's own numbers, weeks instead of daily streaks, no account, export from day one). Pass 2 is a good design system but contradicts pass 1 in four places that matter for retention: it puts "current streak" and "best streak" stats beside a 12-week daily heat strip (both passes list streak counters as an anti-pattern), it adds confetti that pass 1 bans, it moves the install prompt to the second session while pass 1 puts it after the first finish, and it bans dual y-axes while pass 1 proposes an e1RM line overlaid on the weight chart. Both passes over-invest before the loop is proven: PNG share cards, a URL-fragment friend comparison, a laptop keyboard table, a nine-template review matrix and a local analytics pipeline add two to three weeks of work for five to ten friends whose behaviour the developer can read in the group chat. Both passes miss the three things most likely to lose a friend in weeks two to four: no way to edit the routine (so the suggested session stops being theirs), iOS deleting a non-installed site's storage after seven days (so a friend loses two weeks of logs and never returns), and no honest expectation-setting for how slowly recomp shows (so the flat scale reads as failure before the bar has had time to move). Build the twelve items below, show the user the rendered draft before any component is written, and treat everything that needs a canvas, a second person's data or a sidebar as version 1.1.

## Build in this order

1. Three-tap logging loop: home card with one suggested session and one Start button, every set prefilled from the last time that exercise was done (weight, reps, rest), tick per set, per-exercise stepper increments, rest timer that auto-starts on the tick and counts from a stored end timestamp, wake lock while a session is open: The previous-values-plus-tick pattern is the most used feature in Strong and Hevy, and every other mechanic is downstream of whether logging takes 20 seconds in the gym.
2. Never lose a set: autosave every tap to IndexedDB, a resume pill on every screen while a session is live, undo toasts instead of confirm modals, and the ability to edit any past set or weigh-in: One lost or uncorrectable session ends the relationship, and trust in the data is the precondition for every chart the app will later show.
3. The routine is the user's: starter plans with rep ranges for beginners, a one-minute build-my-own path for friends who already train, swap an exercise, add or remove a set, rename, custom exercises, and a picker with aliases and body-part filter: Neither pass says how a user edits the plan, and the home card's suggestion stops being opened the week it suggests a machine their gym does not have.
4. Data durability on iOS: install banner after the first finished workout with the honest reason (Safari can delete a non-installed site's data after seven days without a visit), navigator.storage.persist after the first save, JSON export and import visible from day one, a single 'last backup: 34 days ago' line in the review footer instead of a weekly nag: A friend who loses two weeks of logs never comes back, and the fear of losing months of data stops others from starting.
5. Progress at the point of logging: grey 'last: 60 kg x 8' label, gold tick with a 400 ms inline PR badge (one per set, the most impressive of weight, reps, e1RM, volume), first-time records labelled on workout one, and an e1RM sparkline on each exercise page: Visible progress every session is the strongest retention signal in the research, and in recomp the bar moves weeks before the scale does.
6. Sunday review built only from logged data, states its own basis, gives a thin week a thin review, asks for one free-text line and quotes it back next week, pre-chooses next week's sessions, and ships with three wording states (not enough data yet, consistency first, recomp signal) gated on four weeks and six weigh-ins before any diet or trend verdict; one .ics event with an alarm for review day: The weekly ritual is the loop that beats gamification and costs templates rather than infrastructure, while a confident wrong verdict on week-two noise does more damage than no verdict.
7. Share the review as text through the Web Share API from day one (fallback: copy to clipboard), with a PNG card added only after the text version has been posted in the group twice: The group chat is the only reminder system a serverless PWA has, and a text share costs an hour against three days of canvas, iOS font and Web Share file fights for an image whose uptake is unproven.
8. Recomp chart: trend weight (exponentially weighted over interpolated days, half-life about three weigh-ins) as the solid line with faded raw dots, the most-trained lift's e1RM as a second small panel sharing the x axis, and an optional monthly waist measurement on the weight panel: 'Is anything happening?' is the recomp quit question; flat weight beside rising strength and a shrinking waist is the only honest yes without photos, and stacked panels avoid the dual y-axis the design pass itself bans.
9. Consistency in weeks only: the user picks a weekly target (default 3), the home card shows '2 of 3', and Progress shows a row of the last 12 weeks as chips (hit, partial, off); no run count, no grace accounting, no daily anything: A rolling row makes a missed week a grey chip that scrolls off, so there is nothing to reset, nothing to explain and no grace logic to build.
10. Return path: quiet-week copy that names the next session and nothing else, 'Welcome back. Start light?' after 14 days away with weights prefilled at minus 10 percent and one-tap restore, a pause toggle for travel and illness that marks the week as off: The week after a gap is where fitness apps lose people for good, and this is a handful of strings and one multiplier.
11. Rest-day card defaults to 'Next planned: Thursday, Upper A' and shows protein only after the user has set up favourite foods; diet is protein-only quick add with favourites, recents, 'same as yesterday' and a grams-only fallback, calories opt-in: Food logging is the highest-abandonment feature in the category; protein-only keeps the recomp signal, while '0 of 140 g' on every rest day is a guilt line for a friend who never asked to track food.
12. Progression hint: after two sessions at the top of the rep range on every set, prefill the next session one increment higher with a 'suggested' label and one-tap reject: It turns 'last time' into 'this time', which is the moment the app feels like a coach, for about a day of work and only from the user's own data.

## Dropped

- Calendar heat strip (12 weeks by 7 days, intensity by session length) with 'current streak', 'total sessions' and 'best streak' stats beside it: It is a daily-streak counter by another name and contradicts both passes' own anti-pattern lists; a friend who trains twice a week sees ten empty squares a fortnight and reads it as failure.
- Any daily streak, best-streak or missed-day number anywhere in the app: The first break becomes the churn event; the 12-week chip row and the weekly target carry consistency without a reset moment.
- Rest-timer Notification permission request: iOS suspends page timers in the background and no scheduled-notification API exists, so it cannot fire when it would matter, and the permission prompt is a cost paid before value; vibration, sound, title mirroring and the resume pill cover it.
- Weekly backup-reminder nag: A recurring reminder to export is noise that trains dismissal; one dated 'last backup' line in the review footer says the same thing without nagging.
- 'Protein today: 0 of 140 g' as the default rest-day home card: For a friend who did not opt into food tracking it is a daily zero on the home screen, which is exactly the guilt machine pass 1 argues against elsewhere.
- Friend comparison view in any form for version 1 ('you both showed up 3 times' included): A comparison of two is a leaderboard of two, and the weakest friend is the one retention is about; the group chat does comparison in human terms for free.
- Diet or sleep verdicts in the review ('check protein and sleep this week') before four weeks and six weigh-ins: Two weigh-ins cannot separate water from fat, and a confident wrong sentence from the app costs more trust than silence.
- Automatic grace-week accounting, consecutive-week run counts and the two-zero-weeks pause rule: Rules the user must learn to understand their own consistency number are complexity in service of a metric that should not exist; the rolling chip row needs none of them.
- e1RM line overlaid on the body-weight chart in a second faded series: Different units on one axis is the dual y-axis pass 2 bans, and the chart that answers the recomp question needs to be unambiguous; two stacked panels with a shared x axis do the job.
- Confetti burst on the first PR of a session: Pass 1 bans it, pass 2 adds it; the gold tick and the PR toast already carry the moment and cost nothing.
- Install prompt after the second session (pass 2's timing): On iOS the storage-eviction clock starts at the first visit, so the prompt belongs right after the first finished workout, once, and never again once standalone mode is detected.
- Rest-day 'five favourite protein foods' step during onboarding minute three: Three screens before the first workout is already the limit; ask for favourites the first time the user opens the Eat tab instead.

## Missing from the passes, now required

- Routine editing and an existing-lifter path: swap, add, remove, rename, custom exercise, build-my-own in under a minute; the starter plan must be optional, not the only door
- iOS storage eviction named as a retention precondition: Safari can clear a non-installed site's IndexedDB after seven days without a visit, so install timing, storage.persist and visible export are one feature, not three
- Honest recomp timescale set once at onboarding and repeated in weeks one to three: strength shows in three to four weeks, the mirror in eight to twelve, and the scale is not the scoreboard
- A monthly waist measurement as the cheap body-composition proxy; pass 1 defers photos and neither pass mentions a tape measure, which is the one recomp signal the scale cannot give
- Edit history after the fact (past sets, past weigh-ins, past sessions) and the last three sessions of an exercise one tap from the set row, not just a single 'last' label
- Exercise search quality: aliases (pulldown, lat pulldown, RDL, Romanian deadlift), body-part filter, recents and favourites first; a failed mid-session search is a logged-on-paper session
- Data minimums that gate every review sentence, and a trend smoother defined for weekly weigh-ins: a 7-day EMA over weekly points (pass 2) is just the raw points, so use an exponentially weighted average over interpolated days with a half-life of about three weigh-ins (pass 1's direction)
- Rep ranges inside the starter templates, without which the progression hint and 'top of range' logic cannot fire
- A finish screen that takes under five seconds and can be skipped; the finish step is where many apps add the friction that makes people stop pressing Finish and lose the session
- The phone-and-laptop story stated in the UI: in version 1 the phone is the source of truth and the laptop reads an import, so nobody logs on both and loses a week to the wrong copy
- A measured speed budget before friends get the link: cold open to first logged set under 10 seconds on a mid-range Android, timed by hand, not assumed from the bundle size
- The developer seeds the Sunday norm with a message to the group explaining the card ritual; the app cannot create a social norm on its own and pass 1 assumes it will
- Review the rendered design draft with the user before building any component, as they asked; the draft's accent, density and review-card tone are decisions, not defaults

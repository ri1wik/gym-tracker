# Nutrition data and the food area

Reference material for builders. PLAN.md wins on any conflict.

## Recommendation

Seed the shared food catalogue from USDA FoodData Central SR Legacy entries (public domain, CC0, every generic staple carries the full vitamin and mineral panel) and take branded items such as Isopure and Pintola from the user's own labels and Open Food Facts with a "label data, micronutrients partial" flag and an optional generic micronutrient fill. Keep the Indian Food Composition Tables 2017 out of the shipped catalogue until NIN answers a permission email, because the book's own front matter says no part may be stored or reproduced electronically for creating a product without prior written permission; INDB inherits that restriction and ships no licence of its own. Run USDA search through one Supabase Edge Function that holds the API key (the GitHub Pages bundle is public, and data.gov deactivates keys found in public code) and call Open Food Facts straight from the browser, where CORS is open, no key is needed, and the per-IP rate limit then applies per friend instead of per function. Store 30 fixed nutrient ids per 100 g as integers with null meaning unknown, score the seven-day average against ICMR-NIN 2020 RDA by sex, and show protein, calories, fibre, iron, calcium, vitamin D and B12 by default with the full panel one tap away. Budget seven evening sessions and fence the scope so the Food tab stays a feeder for the weekly review rather than a second app.

## Data sources, verified today (8 Oct 2026)

- USDA FoodData Central: data is public domain under CC0 1.0, attribution requested but not required. API key is free (api.data.gov signup). Default limit 1,000 requests per hour per IP; exceeding it blocks the key for an hour; DEMO_KEY returns x-ratelimit-limit: 10, so never ship with it. CORS confirmed open: a request with Origin ri1wik.github.io returned access-control-allow-origin: *. Live check of fdcId 171077 (chicken breast raw, SR Legacy) returned 129 nutrients including vitamins A RAE, C, D (mcg and IU), E, K, B1, B2, B3, B5, B6, folate DFE, B12, calcium, iron, magnesium, phosphorus, potassium, sodium, zinc, copper, manganese, selenium. Iodine is not in that panel and is sparse across USDA generally.
- USDA caveat found live: the Foundation entry for chicken breast (fdcId 2646170) carries minerals and macros but no vitamins. Rule for the seed script: prefer SR Legacy (dataType filter SR Legacy) for the panel, use Foundation only when SR Legacy lacks the food, and record which one was used. Energy ids differ: 1008 on SR Legacy, 2047 and 2048 (Atwater general and specific) on Foundation; take 1008 or 2048.
- Open Food Facts: database under ODbL, contents under the Database Contents Licence, photos CC BY-SA. Attribution is required; share-alike applies if a merged or adapted database is used publicly, so tag every OFF-derived row with its source and keep the catalogue exportable under ODbL. No API key; a custom User-Agent is mandatory. Limits: 15 product reads per minute per IP, 10 search calls per minute per IP, explicit warning not to use search for type-ahead, IP ban on abuse. CORS confirmed open (access-control-allow-origin: *). Live Indian coverage: 41 Pintola products on in.openfoodfacts.org and 19 Isopure hits worldwide. Micronutrient reality: Pintola entries carry macros, salt and sometimes iron, magnesium, calcium, potassium, vitamin E; the US Isopure Zero Carb entry (0089094021153) carries a full label panel with 20 vitamins and minerals; the UK Isopure entries carry macros only. OFF stores per-100 g nutrient values in grams (vitamin-c_100g 0.012 means 12 mg), with sodium derivable from salt divided by 2.5 and energy sometimes only in kJ; the normaliser must convert.
- IFCT 2017 (ICMR-NIN): 528 foods, 151 components, raw foods only (the few cooked rows are boiled eggs); dal, roti, sabzi and curries are not in it. Front matter, verbatim: "The use and dissemination of the data in this book is encouraged. This publication can be reproduced for personal use with full acknowledgment of the source. However, no part of this publication can be stored or reproduced in any electronic format for creating a product without the prior written permission of the National Institute of Nutrition, Hyderabad." Contacts printed in the book: ifct2017@gmail.com and nin@ap.nic.in. No official CSV or API; FAO lists it as PDF plus a mobile app. Coverage: carotenoids, vitamins D2 and D3, E, K1 and K2, B1, B2, B3, B5, B6, biotin, folate, C, and minerals including calcium, iron, magnesium, phosphorus, potassium, sodium, zinc, copper, manganese, selenium, chromium, molybdenum. It does not carry vitamin B12 or iodine as analysed values. Safe use: personal lookups while developing, a permission request for anything shipped, and never a transcription into the public repo.
- nodef/ifct2017 on GitHub and npm: the book's tables as CSV, SQL and a JS package; the maintainers relicensed from MIT to AGPL-3.0 in 2025. Their licence covers their packaging, not NIN's rights in the numbers, and AGPL on imported code would bind the app's own licence. Do not import it into the app; at most use it on the laptop to compare values.
- INDB (Indian Nutrient Databank, Current Developments in Nutrition 2024, Jaacks and co-authors): article is CC BY 4.0; the data lives in github.com/lindsayjaacks/Indian-Nutrient-Databank-INDB- as INDB.xlsx (1,095 foods, 1,014 recipes) with a Stata script, and the repository has no LICENSE file. Food values come from IFCT 2017 and 2004 with UK and US fill-ins, so NIN's clause applies transitively. Recipes are plain ingredient sums per 100 g and per serving; retention factors only in a sensitivity analysis; yield factors not applied. No B12, no iodine. Safe use: read its recipe ingredient lists and serving sizes as the model for the shipped presets and compute nutrients from USDA-sourced ingredients in the app.
- Which source reliably has what: USDA SR Legacy has everything on the 30-id list except iodine. Open Food Facts has macros, salt and sugars almost always, vitamins and minerals only when the label printed them. IFCT has the full panel minus B12 and iodine, with Indian cultivars and species. Iodine has no reliable source anywhere; store it as unknown and consider an iodised-salt assumption later.

## Where each staple comes from

- USDA SR Legacy, full panel, safe to vendor as a committed JSON seed: chicken breast raw (171077) and roasted; whole egg raw and hard-boiled; egg white raw; lentils raw and boiled (masoor); mung beans raw and boiled (moong); pigeon peas raw (toor); chickpeas raw and boiled (chana); kidney beans raw and boiled (rajma); oats rolled; rice white raw and cooked; whole wheat flour (atta); whole milk; plain yogurt (curd); natural peanut butter (as the micronutrient fill for Pintola); prawns raw; goat meat raw (mutton); generic fish entries as nearest-generic stand-ins for rohu, catla, pomfret and basa.
- Label plus Open Food Facts, panel flagged partial: Isopure whey isolate per scoop (the US entry prints 25 g protein per 31 g scoop with a vitamin and mineral blend; the Indian tub label may differ, so the user types from the tub); Pintola peanut butter per 32 g label serving, 16 g per tablespoon; packaged paneer and curd (Amul, Mother Dairy on OFF); soya chunks (Nutrela label for macros, USDA defatted soy flour as the fill, flagged estimated).
- Needs IFCT, so stand-in now and swap when permission arrives: Indian fish species, buffalo milk, desi dal cultivars, Indian wheat atta. Error from the USDA stand-in is small for dals and eggs, larger for fish species and buffalo milk; the row carries nearest_generic: true so the UI can say so.
- Computed as recipes, not looked up: dal cooked per katori (raw dal, water, oil, salt, yield grams), roti (atta, water, optional ghee), chicken curry, rajma masala, egg bhurji. Cooked values that are not a recipe (plain boiled lentils, cooked rice) come from the USDA cooked entries directly.
- Indian portion defaults, all editable and weighable once: katori 150 g for dal, sabzi, curd and cooked rice; roti 40 g cooked from 30 g atta; scoop 31 g; tablespoon 16 g (Pintola); whole egg 50 g, egg white 33 g; chicken breast, fish, mutton, prawns per 100 g raw with cooked twins at about 70 percent yield; glass of milk 250 g; paneer cube 20 g, piece 100 g; soya chunks 50 g dry. Every portion carries a state (raw, cooked, as_served) so raw and cooked never double count.

## Catalogue architecture, keys and CORS

- Tables: food (id, source in usda, off, label, recipe, user; source_id; unique on source plus source_id; name; name_alt text[] for dal, daal, dhal and Hindi spellings; brand; panel enum full, partial, macros; micro_from food id for generic fill; n jsonb of integers; generated kcal and protein_mg columns for sorting; nearest_generic bool; licence text for attribution; created_by). portion (food_id, label, grams, state, is_default). favourite (user_id, food_id, portion_id, last_grams, use_count, last_used_at). recipe (owner or shared, name, items jsonb of food_id and grams, yield_g, servings, n per 100 g, panel). food_log (user_id, date_key YYYY-MM-DD local, food_id or recipe_id, grams, n_snapshot jsonb) so later catalogue edits never rewrite history.
- Row level security: every authenticated user reads the whole catalogue; user-added foods and recipes are writable only by their creator and readable by all once is_public is true; favourites and food_log are owner-only. Seeded rows have created_by null and are read-only.
- Search path, cache first: the client queries Postgres directly through PostgREST with pg_trgm on name and name_alt, which answers almost every search with zero function calls. Only an explicit "search online" tap invokes the Edge Function food-search, which holds FDC_API_KEY as a Supabase secret, calls USDA with dataType SR Legacy and Foundation, normalises to the 30-id model, upserts into food with source usda, and returns the rows. Supabase Free includes 500,000 invocations a month and a 10 second execution cap; a friends group will use a few hundred. Cost: zero.
- Why not call USDA from the browser: CORS would allow it, but GitHub Pages on a free account means a public repository, so any key in the bundle is public, and data.gov states that publicly posted keys are deactivated. The function is the only place the key exists.
- Open Food Facts from the browser: barcode lookup via /api/v2/product/{code} with fields=product_name,brands,nutriments and the mandatory custom User-Agent, normalised client-side and inserted through RLS as source off. No type-ahead against OFF search; the 10 per minute search limit is reserved for an explicit "search Open Food Facts" tap. Routing OFF through the function would pool every friend behind one egress IP and hit the 15 per minute product limit sooner.
- Deduplication: source plus source_id is the hard key; for user-added rows, a soft check proposes an existing food when name trigram similarity is high and kcal and protein per 100 g are within 5 percent. Favourites are per user and ordered by use_count then last_used_at.
- Attribution lives in the food detail sheet: "USDA FoodData Central" for usda rows, "Open Food Facts, ODbL" with a link for off rows, "from label" for label rows. Keep an export of off rows possible to satisfy share-alike cheaply.

## Nutrient model and unit handling

- Fixed list of 30 nutrient ids in the domain module, nothing dynamic: energy_kcal, protein, carbs, fat, fibre, sugar, sat_fat, vit_a_rae, vit_c, vit_d, vit_e, vit_k, b1, b2, b3, b5, b6, b9_dfe, b12, calcium, iron, magnesium, phosphorus, potassium, sodium, zinc, copper, manganese, selenium, iodine.
- Canonical integer units per 100 g, matching the product's integer rule: energy in kcal; the six macros in mg; every vitamin and mineral in mcg. Potassium in a dried legume is about 1,400,000 mcg and carbohydrate at 100 g is 100,000,000 mg-scale equivalents, both inside int4. Vitamin D stored in mcg (IU divided by 40). Vitamin A in mcg RAE; an IU-only label converts at 0.3 mcg per IU for retinol and is flagged approximate. Vitamin E in mcg alpha-tocopherol (IU times 670 for natural, 450 for synthetic, flagged approximate). Niacin in mcg NE, folate in mcg DFE.
- Null means unknown and is never summed as zero. Day totals carry, per nutrient, the grams of logged food that had a known value, so the panel can print "based on 82 percent of today's food" and hatch the unknown share of a bar. panel on the food is derived: full when all 30 known or marked true zero, macros when only the seven macros are known, partial otherwise.
- USDA id mapping for the seed script (verified live for minerals and macros, vitamin ids to be re-checked on the first SR Legacy fetch): 1008 or 2048 energy, 1003 protein, 1005 carbs, 1004 fat, 1079 fibre, 2000 sugars, 1258 saturated fat, 1106 vitamin A RAE, 1162 vitamin C, 1114 vitamin D mcg, 1109 vitamin E, 1185 vitamin K, 1165 B1, 1166 B2, 1167 B3, 1170 B5, 1175 B6, 1190 folate DFE, 1178 B12, 1087 calcium, 1089 iron, 1090 magnesium, 1091 phosphorus, 1092 potassium, 1093 sodium, 1095 zinc, 1098 copper, 1101 manganese, 1103 selenium, 1100 iodine.
- Open Food Facts key mapping: proteins, carbohydrates, fat, fiber, sugars, saturated-fat, energy-kcal (fall back to energy in kJ divided by 4.184), sodium or salt divided by 2.5, calcium, iron, magnesium, phosphorus, potassium, zinc, copper, manganese, selenium, iodine, vitamin-a, vitamin-c, vitamin-d, vitamin-e, vitamin-k, vitamin-b1, vitamin-b2, vitamin-pp (niacin), pantothenic-acid, vitamin-b6, folates, vitamin-b12; all _100g values are grams and must be scaled to mg or mcg.
- Generic fill: a label row may point micro_from at a USDA generic (Pintola to natural peanut butter, soya chunks to defatted soy flour). The fill copies only the vitamin and mineral ids the label left null, scaled by the label's protein ratio, and the row shows "micronutrients estimated from generic peanut butter". Pure-whey products get no fill; whey isolate without a label panel stays unknown rather than borrowing milk values.
- Codable tags computed from the panel: "source of X" when a default portion gives at least 15 percent of the RDA, "high in X" at 30 percent. These feed the protein helper and the day panel's "what covers iron" hint without any extra data.

## Reference intakes and the daily panel

- Use ICMR-NIN 2020 as the 100 percent mark, by sex from the profile, because the brief note on nin.res.in gives adult RDA and EAR for the nutrients that matter on this diet. Men, RDA 2020 (EAR in brackets): calcium 1000 mg (800), magnesium 440 mg (370), iron 19 mg (11), zinc 17 mg (14), iodine 150 mcg (95), B1 1.8 mg (1.5), B2 2.5 mg (2.1), B3 18 mg (15), B6 2.4 mg (2.1), folate 300 mcg DFE (250), B12 2.2 mcg (2.0), vitamin C 80 mg (65), vitamin A 1000 mcg (460), vitamin D 600 IU, 15 mcg (400 IU). Women: calcium 1000 (800), magnesium 370 (310), iron 29 (15), zinc 13 (11), iodine 150 (95), B1 1.7 (1.4), B2 2.4 (2.0), B3 14 (12), B6 1.9 (1.6), folate 220 (180), B12 2.2 (2.0), C 65 (55), A 840 (390), D 600 IU (400). Protein RDA 0.83 g per kg, EAR 0.66, and 1.0 g per kg for cereal-based diets; the product's 2.0 g per kg recomp target overrides this on purpose.
- Nutrients the brief note does not list take US DRI values, labelled as such in the targets table: phosphorus 700 mg, potassium 3400 mg men and 2600 women (AI), sodium 1500 mg AI with 2300 mg as the reduction ceiling, vitamin E 15 mg, vitamin K 120 mcg men and 90 women (AI), B5 5 mg AI, copper 900 mcg, manganese 2.3 mg men and 1.8 women (AI), selenium 55 mcg. A settings toggle switches the whole table to US DRI for friends who prefer it. Pregnancy and lactation are out of scope.
- NIN itself recommends EAR for planning an individual's menu and RDA for covering deficiency, so the bar runs to RDA with a tick at EAR. Reading: at or above RDA is covered, between EAR and RDA is adequate, below EAR is low. Three neutral shades, no red, no notification, no daily nag.
- The primary number is the seven-day average, not today, because single-day micronutrient intake is noisy and a dal day and a chicken day differ by design. Today's figures sit under it. Nothing is judged until five of the last seven days are logged and panel coverage is above 80 percent; before that the row reads "to unlock: log 5 days" in the agreed to-unlock style.
- Default panel, always visible on the Food tab day summary: protein, calories, fibre, iron, calcium, vitamin D, B12. These are the gaps of a dal, roti, curd and chicken diet. Behind the "full panel" tap: all 30 with source coverage. Magnesium and zinc are promoted to the default row automatically if their seven-day average has sat below EAR for two weeks running.
- Weekly review hook: micronutrients contribute at most one candidate for the single focus, chosen only when a default-row nutrient has a seven-day average below EAR with coverage above 80 percent, and it is phrased as a food suggestion ("two katori rajma or a 100 g curd portion covers it"), never as a deficiency claim.

## Recipes

- A recipe is a list of (food, grams) plus yield_g (cooked weight on the scale) and servings. Nutrients per 100 g cooked = sum of ingredient nutrients divided by yield_g; per serving = per 100 g times yield_g over servings. Water added as an ingredient keeps the sum honest when the user does not weigh the pot; yield_g is required when water is omitted.
- Retention factors are skipped in v1, the same choice INDB made as its default; the recipe sheet says "vitamin C and B vitamins are upper bounds, cooking loses some". Panel of a recipe is the weakest panel of its ingredients, so a dal built from USDA lentils, oil, salt and onion stays full.
- Four shipped presets, built from USDA-sourced ingredients with portions in katori: moong dal tadka (moong, water, oil, onion, tomato, salt, yield about 600 g for 4 katori), rajma masala, chicken curry (breast, onion, tomato, curd, oil), and roti as a recipe (30 g atta plus water, yield 40 g) so a roti count maps to atta grams. INDB's recipe lists are the reference for proportions; the numbers are recomputed in the app, not copied.
- Recipes are per user by default and can be shared to the catalogue with one toggle; shared recipes are read-only to others and can be cloned. A recipe logs like a food: pick, katori count, done.

## Logging flow and the protein helper

- Protein-first stays: the Food tab header is the protein ring with grams remaining and calories remaining as the second number; the micronutrient summary is a chip under it, not a column in the list.
- Two taps for a favourite: tap the favourite tile (it carries the last portion and shows protein and kcal for that portion), tap confirm. The portion stepper is on the confirm sheet for the 20 percent of cases where the amount changed. New foods: search, pick, choose portion, confirm, then it becomes a favourite automatically after the second use.
- Micronutrient panel is one tap from two places: the day summary chip opens the 30-id panel for the day; a logged row's detail opens the per-food panel with its source and coverage line. Neither appears on the logging path.
- Unknowns are visible but quiet: a small "2 items without micronutrient data" line under the day panel, tapping it lists them with a "add from label" shortcut.
- Protein maximisation helper, rebuilt for this food set: given protein remaining and calories remaining for the day, rank the user's favourites plus the seeded staples by protein per 100 kcal, keep those whose default portion fits the remaining calories, and show the top three with portion and the protein they add (egg whites, chicken breast, Isopure scoop, soya chunks, low-fat curd, masoor dal typically lead). A fourth slot shows the option that also closes the day's weakest default-row micronutrient, using the "source of" tags (for example paneer for calcium, rajma for iron, eggs for B12). The helper fires only when protein remaining is above 20 g after the last logged meal or on tap; it is not a notification.

## Build effort and the second-app risk

- Session 1: nutrient ids, units, conversion and summation functions in the pure domain module with Vitest; the seed script that fetches about 40 USDA staples with the laptop's key, normalises them and commits the JSON (CC0, so safe in the public repo).
- Session 2: Supabase tables, RLS, seed load, portions and favourites, food_log with snapshots.
- Session 3: Food tab logging: favourites grid, portion sheet, two-tap confirm, protein ring, day list.
- Session 4: catalogue search with pg_trgm, the food-search Edge Function with the FDC secret and upsert cache, label entry form, OFF barcode lookup by typed code.
- Session 5: day panel, targets table by sex, seven-day average, coverage line, full-panel sheet, to-unlock states.
- Session 6: recipe builder with yield, four presets, sharing toggle.
- Session 7: protein helper, weekly review hook for one micronutrient focus, attribution text, polish. Total seven sessions, eight with fixes; sessions 1 to 3 ship first so protein logging with full panels is live before the training loop finishes, 4 to 7 follow.
- Scope fence, written into the plan: no camera barcode scanning in v1 (BarcodeDetector later), no photo food recognition, no meal timing, no restaurant database, no AI portion guessing, no per-meal targets, one search function only, recipes only from catalogue foods, and every Food tab feature must feed a weekly review signal or it is cut.

## Must have

- Fixed 30-nutrient schema in the domain module, integers per 100 g (kcal, mg for macros, mcg for vitamins and minerals), null meaning unknown, never summed as zero, with a per-food panel flag (full, partial, macros) and a source plus source_id on every row.
- Committed USDA SR Legacy seed of about 40 generic staples (dals raw and boiled, chicken breast raw and cooked, eggs, oats, rice, atta, milk, curd, peanut butter, prawns, goat meat, fish stand-ins) with full vitamin and mineral panels, fetched by a script with the laptop key, never with DEMO_KEY.
- Label entry form for Isopure, Pintola, paneer, curd and soya chunks with required macros, optional micronutrients, and the micro_from generic fill flagged as estimated.
- Supabase catalogue tables with RLS (shared read, creator-only write, owner-only favourites and log), portions with Indian units and raw or cooked state, food_log rows that snapshot nutrients.
- One Edge Function food-search holding FDC_API_KEY as a secret, cache-first against Postgres, upserting normalised USDA results; Open Food Facts barcode lookups straight from the browser with a custom User-Agent, no type-ahead against OFF.
- Day panel scored against ICMR-NIN 2020 RDA by sex with EAR ticks, US DRI fallback for the nutrients the NIN brief note omits, seven-day average as the primary number, coverage line for unknowns, default row of protein, calories, fibre, iron, calcium, vitamin D and B12, full panel behind one tap, no red and no notifications.
- Recipe builder with ingredients, yield grams and servings, panel inherited from ingredients, and four shipped presets (moong dal tadka, rajma masala, chicken curry, roti).
- Two-tap favourite logging with last portion remembered, and the protein helper ranked by protein per 100 kcal within remaining calories.
- Attribution text on food detail for USDA and Open Food Facts rows, and IFCT 2017 excluded from the shipped catalogue until NIN's written permission is in hand.

- Swap nearest-generic stand-ins (Indian fish species, buffalo milk, desi dal cultivars) for IFCT 2017 values once NIN grants permission, keyed by IFCT food codes.

- The remaining ICMR-NIN 2020 targets (phosphorus, potassium, sodium, E, K, B5, copper, manganese, selenium) from the NIN short summary of the RDA book, replacing the US DRI fallback.
- An iodised-salt assumption toggle that credits iodine per gram of salt logged, since no food source carries iodine reliably.
- Per-user target overrides (for example an iron target set by a doctor) and a US DRI switch per friend.
- Check the 2026 Indian Packaged Foods Nutritional Composition Dataset on Mendeley Data for brands missing from Open Food Facts, only if its licence reads CC BY; expect macros only.
- ODbL export of the Open Food Facts derived rows if the catalogue ever becomes visible beyond the friends group.

## Risks

- NIN never answers the permission email: Indian species and cultivars stay on USDA stand-ins flagged nearest_generic; the error is small for dals and eggs, larger for fish species and buffalo milk, and the UI must keep saying so.
- Transcribing IFCT or INDB values into the public repo or catalogue before permission would breach the clause quoted in the book; INDB's CC BY article licence does not cover the xlsx, which has no licence file and derives from IFCT.
- ODbL share-alike on merged Open Food Facts rows: a friends-only app is low exposure, but the rows must stay tagged and exportable, and the attribution line must be present.
- Micronutrient totals mislead when several logged foods are label-only; if null is ever treated as zero the panel will report false lows and drive a wrong weekly focus. The coverage line and the 80 percent gate are the guard.
- Supabase free projects pause after a week without requests; a holiday can leave the app offline until restored from the dashboard. The 4-day weigh-in cadence keeps it alive in normal use.
- USDA key handling: DEMO_KEY (limit 10) left in a script, or a burst over 1,000 per hour, blocks the key for an hour; cache-first and the committed seed make live calls rare.
- Raw versus cooked double counting (100 g raw chicken logged as cooked, or both) inflates protein by about 40 percent; every portion carries a state and the seed holds cooked twins.
- Recipes without a yield figure produce wrong per-katori values; the builder requires yield grams or water as an ingredient.
- Importing nodef/ifct2017 (AGPL-3.0 since 2025) into the app would bind the app's licence and still not clear NIN's clause.
- Second-app creep: barcode cameras, photo logging and meal plans each look like one evening and together double the food area; the scope fence and the rule that every feature feeds a weekly review signal are the only brake.
